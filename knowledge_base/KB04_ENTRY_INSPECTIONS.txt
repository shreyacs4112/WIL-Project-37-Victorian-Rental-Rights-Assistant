SUMMARY
This document summarises Victorian rules about when rental providers and their agents can enter a rented property. Entry is only permitted for specific lawful reasons and must follow the required notice and timing rules.

1. RIGHT OF ENTRY

Rental providers and their agents have a legal right to enter a rental property in certain circumstances.

Renters must generally allow entry when the rental provider or agent is legally entitled to enter and has provided the required notice.

Entry can include entering the property as a whole, including outdoor areas such as gardens.

2. REASONS FOR ENTRY

A rental provider or agent may enter a rental property for reasons including:

- General or routine inspection
- Repairs or other legal responsibilities
- Showing the property to prospective renters
- Showing the property to prospective buyers or lenders
- Having the property valued
- Taking photographs or videos for advertising
- Investigating whether the renter has breached legal or rental agreement obligations
- Certain inspections connected with VCAT family violence proceedings

The reason for entry determines the minimum notice period.

3. MINIMUM NOTICE PERIODS

The minimum written notice generally required is:

- General or routine inspection: 7 days
- Repairs or other legal responsibilities: 24 hours
- Showing the property to prospective renters, buyers or lenders: 48 hours
- Property valuation: 7 days
- Taking photographs or videos for advertising: 7 days
- Believing the renter has breached their obligations: 24 hours
- Certain VCAT family violence proceedings: 24 hours

4. WHEN ENTRY CAN OCCUR

A rental provider or agent can generally enter between 8 am and 6 pm on any day except a public holiday.

Entry outside these hours is only permitted if the renter agrees.

The agreement to enter outside the normal hours must be made no more than 7 days before the intended entry.

5. WHEN A RENTER CAN REFUSE ENTRY

A renter can refuse entry in circumstances such as:

- Entry is outside the permitted hours without the renter's agreement
- The day is a public holiday
- Required written notice was not provided
- Notice was not provided within the required timeframe
- A routine inspection has already occurred within the previous 6 months
- A prospective-renter inspection is being requested outside the permitted period

A renter may also seek an order from VCAT to limit entry times where there are good reasons for doing so.

6. GENERAL OR ROUTINE INSPECTIONS

A general inspection is used to check the condition of the rental property and whether the renter is meeting their obligations.

A general inspection:

- Cannot normally occur during the first 3 months of the rental agreement
- Can occur no more than once every 6 months
- Requires at least 7 days' notice

The rental provider or agent can inspect all areas of the property during a general inspection.

7. SHOWING THE PROPERTY TO PROSPECTIVE RENTERS

A rental provider or agent can show the property to prospective renters when the current renter has given notice that they intend to leave or when the rental provider has given a notice to vacate.

An open inspection for prospective renters must occur no more than 21 days before the end of the rental agreement.

Prospective renters generally cannot be shown the property more than twice per week or for longer than one hour at a time unless the current renter agrees to different arrangements.

8. SHOWING THE PROPERTY TO BUYERS OR LENDERS

A rental provider or agent can show the property to prospective buyers when the property is going to be sold.

The renter must generally have been told that the owner intends to sell the property at least 14 days before the inspection.

The rental provider must make reasonable efforts to agree with the renter about suitable inspection days and times.

Prospective buyer inspections generally cannot occur more than twice per week or for longer than one hour each time unless the renter agrees otherwise.

9. COMPENSATION FOR SALES INSPECTIONS

When a rental provider enters the property to hold a sales inspection for prospective buyers, the renter must generally receive compensation.

The compensation is the greater of:

- Half a day's rent
- $30

10. PHOTOGRAPHS AND VIDEOS FOR ADVERTISING

A rental provider or agent may enter to take photographs or videos for advertising after giving the required notice.

Renters can object in writing when photographs or videos would reveal certain personal, sensitive, valuable or identifying possessions.

If a renter makes a valid objection, the rental provider or agent must not include those items in the advertising material.

In some circumstances, the renter can request to see advertising photographs or videos before they are used.

11. ENTRY WHEN THE RENTER IS NOT HOME

A rental provider or agent can enter when the renter is not home if valid written notice has been provided or the renter has agreed to the entry.

The renter does not generally need to be present during a lawful entry.

12. RENTERS DO NOT GENERALLY HAVE TO LEAVE

A rental provider may ask a renter to leave during an inspection or property showing, but a renter is generally not required to leave simply because the rental provider or agent is entering lawfully.

13. RENTAL PROVIDERS MUST ACT REASONABLY

A rental provider or agent must exercise entry rights reasonably.

They should not stay longer than necessary for the purpose of the visit unless the renter agrees.

Unreasonable entry or repeated unnecessary visits can be challenged.

A renter may apply to VCAT where entry rights are being misused or legal duties have been breached.

14. DAMAGE DURING ENTRY

If a renter's belongings are damaged or taken while the rental provider or agent is on the property, the renter may be able to apply to VCAT for compensation.

15. FORMS

Consumer Affairs Victoria provides forms including:

- Notice to renter of entry to rented premises
- Notice of intention to sell the property
- Notice of breach of duty to renter of rented premises

16. RELEVANT LAW

Rental provider entry rights and responsibilities are covered by sections 85–91A of the Residential Tenancies Act 1997.
