SUMMARY
This document summarises Victorian rental rules and processes relating to bond claims and refunds at the end of a rental agreement. Rental bonds are managed by the Residential Tenancies Bond Authority (RTBA). When a rental agreement ends, the bond is repaid according to an agreed claim or an applicable order where there is a dispute.

1. BOND CLAIMS AT THE END OF A RENTAL AGREEMENT

When a rental agreement ends, renters and rental providers make a bond claim to the Residential Tenancies Bond Authority (RTBA).

A bond claim tells the RTBA how the bond should be repaid.

Bond money is generally returned to renters when they move out unless there is a valid reason for the rental provider to claim some or all of the bond.

Bonds cannot be claimed to pay rent during an ongoing rental agreement.

2. REASONS A RENTAL PROVIDER MAY CLAIM BOND

A rental provider may claim compensation from the bond because the renter:

- Damaged the property
- Owes rent or other charges after the rental agreement has ended
- Did not leave the property reasonably clean
- Is responsible for missing items, such as locks
- Made certain changes to the property without permission

A rental provider cannot claim bond for fair wear and tear or for damage that was the rental provider's responsibility to repair.

3. FINAL INSPECTION AND CONDITION REPORT

A final inspection is carried out at the end of the rental agreement.

The condition report should be used to compare the condition of the property at the beginning and end of the rental agreement.

The renter must either:

- Be present during the final inspection, or
- Have been given a reasonable opportunity to attend

Photographs taken at the beginning of the rental agreement can also provide evidence of the property's original condition.

4. FAIR WEAR AND TEAR

Rental providers cannot claim bond to cover fair wear and tear.

Fair wear and tear is deterioration resulting from:

- Reasonable use of the property
- Natural environmental forces

Examples may include faded curtains or worn kitchen benchtops.

Damage such as ripped curtains or broken benchtops may be treated differently from fair wear and tear.

If renters and rental providers disagree about whether something is reasonable wear and tear or damage, they can seek dispute resolution.

5. UNPAID RENT OR CHARGES

If a renter owes rent after the rental agreement has ended, the rental provider may claim the amount owed from the bond.

The bond cannot be used to pay rent while the rental agreement is still ongoing.

6. BOND DISPUTES

Sometimes renters and rental providers disagree about how the bond should be distributed.

For example, a renter may believe:

- They did not cause the alleged damage
- The damage existed before the rental agreement
- The issue is fair wear and tear
- The amount claimed by the rental provider is not appropriate

Renters and rental providers are encouraged to negotiate and try to reach an agreement.

If the parties cannot agree, a party may seek assistance through Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria (RDRV) or apply to the Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal (VCAT), depending on the applicable process.

An applicable legally binding order can be submitted to the RTBA, which will distribute the bond according to the order.

7. CLAIMING A BOND THROUGH THE RTBA

Bond claims are made using the RTBA online system.

A rental provider must start a claim with the RTBA within 14 days of the rental agreement ending unless a renter starts the claim first.

Rental providers must be registered with the RTBA to initiate a claim.

8. RENTAL PROVIDER-INITIATED CLAIM

A rental provider will usually start a bond claim after the parties have agreed on how the bond should be repaid.

When the rental provider submits the claim, each renter listed on the bond receives an email notification containing a link to review the claim.

Renters can:

- Review the proposed repayment
- Accept the claim
- Request a change if they believe something is incorrect

Renters must accept the claim within 14 days or the transaction will no longer be available to complete.

If everyone accepts the claim, the RTBA will usually repay the bond within one business day.

9. RENTER-INITIATED BOND CLAIM

Any renter whose name is listed on the bond can start a bond claim after the rental agreement ends.

The renter must authenticate their identity with Service Victoria before starting the claim.

The renter does not need to wait for the rental provider to initiate the bond claim.

When a renter starts a claim, the RTBA notifies the other parties listed on the bond.

Other parties generally have 14 days to contest the claim by making an application to VCAT or RDRV.

If nobody contests the claim, the bond is repaid according to the claim after the response period.

The bond can be repaid sooner if all parties agree.

10. CONTESTING A RENTER-INITIATED CLAIM

If another party disagrees with a renter-initiated bond claim, they can contest it through the applicable dispute process.

The party must act within the relevant response period.

If the claim is not contested within the required period, the RTBA can repay the bond according to the renter-initiated claim.

11. VCAT OR RDRV ORDERS

Where a bond dispute requires a decision, an applicable legally binding order can determine how the bond should be distributed.

An order may be necessary when:

- There is a dispute about the distribution of the bond
- At least one party cannot be contacted

The relevant order or reference can be submitted as part of the RTBA bond-claim process.

Once an applicable order is validated, the RTBA can distribute the bond according to that order.

12. RTBA REVIEW OF DOCUMENTATION

In some circumstances, supporting documentation may need to be attached to a bond claim.

This may occur when:

- An order cannot be automatically validated
- A court other than VCAT has made an order
- Other circumstances require the RTBA to review supporting documentation

The RTBA reviews the submitted documentation before completing the bond transaction.

13. BOND REPAYMENT

During the claim process, people receiving a repayment provide their bank account details.

The RTBA pays the bond into the nominated bank account.

Australian and international bank accounts can be used.

People making a claim should carefully check their banking details because the RTBA does not retain renter banking details for verification.

If valid payment details have not been provided, the RTBA may retain the repayment until a valid payment option is provided.

14. PORTABLE RENTAL BONDS

A bond may be transferred to a new rental property through the Portable Rental Bond Scheme.

The same general bond-dispute process applies to a bond that has been transferred.

The rental provider for the previous property can still make a bond claim after the bond has been transferred.

If money is payable to the previous rental provider and there is not enough bond balance remaining after the transfer, the government may pay the rental provider on the renter's behalf.

That amount then becomes a repayment owed by the renter to the State of Victoria.

15. BOND CLOSURE AFTER A PORTABLE BOND TRANSFER

The RTBA can close a bond when:

- Its balance is $0 after transfer to a new rental property
- The bond is not under dispute
- No claims have been submitted

People listed on the bond are notified before closure.

They generally have 14 days to dispute the closure by making an application to VCAT or RDRV and providing the relevant reference to the RTBA.

16. HOMES VICTORIA BOND LOANS

Bonds funded by Homes Victoria are generally returned in full to Homes Victoria at the end of the rental agreement.

If a rental provider wants to claim some or all of a Homes Victoria-funded bond, they must obtain an applicable VCAT order.

17. RELEVANT LEGISLATION

Consumer Affairs Victoria identifies the following provisions of the Residential Tenancies Act 1997 in relation to bond claims and refunds:

- Section 411AB – Matters that may be subject of bond claim
- Section 411AD – Disputed bond claims
