SUMMARY
This document summarises Victorian rental rules relating to renters ending a residential rental agreement and moving out. It covers giving notice, ending an agreement at the end of its term, leaving early, breaking a rental agreement, and important steps before leaving the property.

1. NOTICE OF INTENTION TO VACATE

A renter who wants to end their rental agreement can:

- Reach an agreement with the rental provider about leaving, or
- Give a formal written notice of intention to vacate.

A notice of intention to vacate is a formal statement that the renter wants to end the rental agreement.

2. LEAVING AT THE END OF A RENTAL AGREEMENT

A renter generally needs to give 28 days' notice if they want to leave at the end of their rental agreement.

For a fixed-term agreement, the renter should ensure the notice does not specify a date earlier than the agreement's end date unless a lawful reason for early termination applies.

Since 25 November 2025, a fixed-term agreement generally becomes a periodic agreement when it ends if neither party enters a new fixed-term agreement and the rental provider does not give a valid notice to vacate.

3. GIVING NOTICE IN WRITING

The renter must give the notice of intention to vacate in writing.

The official Consumer Affairs Victoria form is recommended:

Notice of intention to vacate rented premises by renter.

The notice should clearly state:

- The renter's intention to leave
- The address of the rented premises
- The date the renter intends to leave
- Relevant details required by the form

4. HOW TO DELIVER THE NOTICE

A renter can deliver the notice by:

- Post
- Electronic communication, such as email, where permitted
- Hand delivery to the rental provider

If the notice is posted, the renter should allow enough time for delivery.

Renters should keep evidence that the notice was sent or delivered.

5. LEAVING EARLY WITHOUT BREAKING THE AGREEMENT

There are specific circumstances where a renter can end a rental agreement before the usual end date without breaking the agreement.

The applicable notice period depends on the reason.

Examples include:

- The property becomes unfit for human habitation or is destroyed to an extent that makes it unsafe
- The rental provider breaches certain VCAT orders
- The rental provider repeatedly breaches certain duties
- The rental provider refuses requested disability-related changes
- The renter needs temporary crisis accommodation
- The renter needs special or personal care
- The renter is moving into social housing
- Certain notices to vacate have been given by the rental provider

Some reasons require documentary evidence.

6. NOTICE PERIODS FOR CERTAIN EARLY-ENDING REASONS

Depending on the circumstances, the required notice can be:

- Immediate
- 14 days
- 28 days

The renter should check the specific reason and evidence requirements before relying on a shorter notice period.

7. BREAKING A RENTAL AGREEMENT

Leaving a property before the rental agreement ends without a lawful reason or without the required agreement can amount to breaking the rental agreement.

A renter who breaks the agreement may have to pay reasonable costs caused by the early termination.

Potential costs can include:

- Rent until a new renter moves in or the agreement ends, subject to the law
- Advertising costs
- Reasonable costs of finding a new renter
- Other permitted costs resulting from the early termination

A rental provider cannot simply charge any amount they choose.

8. COSTS A RENTER DOES NOT HAVE TO PAY

A renter generally does not have to pay costs that are unreasonable or unrelated to the early termination.

The rental provider should take reasonable steps to reduce their losses.

A renter can dispute claimed costs if they believe the amount is incorrect or unreasonable.

9. SEVERE HARDSHIP

A renter experiencing severe hardship may apply to VCAT to end a fixed-term rental agreement without having to pay the usual costs associated with breaking the agreement.

Examples of circumstances that may contribute to severe hardship include:

- Significant loss of income
- Serious personal circumstances
- Serious medical circumstances
- Family tragedy

VCAT considers the circumstances of the renter and the rental provider when deciding an application.

10. PREPARING TO MOVE OUT

Before leaving, renters should:

- Clean the property reasonably
- Remove their belongings
- Remove rubbish
- Return all required keys
- Pay rent and other amounts that are lawfully owed
- Complete any required final inspection process
- Keep copies of important rental records
- Take photographs of the property's final condition

Renters should compare the property's final condition with the original condition report.

11. FAIR WEAR AND TEAR

Renters are generally responsible for damage they caused but not for normal fair wear and tear.

Fair wear and tear can result from ordinary use and the natural ageing of a property.

The original condition report and photographs can help determine whether damage existed before the renter moved in or occurred during the tenancy.

12. FINAL INSPECTION

A final inspection can be used to assess the condition of the property when the renter leaves.

The original condition report should be used as evidence when comparing the property's condition at the beginning and end of the rental agreement.

The renter should have a reasonable opportunity to attend the final inspection where required.

13. BOND AFTER MOVING OUT

After the rental agreement ends, the rental bond can be claimed through the Residential Tenancies Bond Authority (RTBA).

The rental provider must generally start a bond claim within 14 days after the rental agreement ends unless the renter starts the claim first.

The renter can initiate the bond claim and can dispute a rental provider's claim.

Fair wear and tear cannot be claimed as damage from the bond.

14. RETURNING KEYS

Renters should return all keys, remotes, access devices and other items that belong to the rental provider.

Failure to return keys or access devices may result in costs where permitted by law.

Renters should keep evidence that keys and access devices were returned.

15. RENT AND FINAL PAYMENTS

Renters remain responsible for rent and other lawful amounts up to the end of the rental agreement or until another lawful arrangement applies.

Rent should not simply be stopped because a renter has given notice.

If there is a dispute about money owed, the renter should seek advice rather than withholding rent without a lawful basis.

16. WITHDRAWING A NOTICE TO VACATE

If a renter gives a notice of intention to vacate and later decides to stay, they should tell the rental provider as soon as possible and provide a written request to withdraw the notice.

The rental provider or agent does not have to agree to withdraw the notice.

If the rental provider does not agree, the renter may apply to VCAT in appropriate circumstances.

Unless VCAT allows the renter to stay longer, the renter must generally leave by the date stated in the original notice.

17. IF THE RENTER DOES NOT LEAVE

If a renter gives notice but does not leave by the required date, the rental provider can apply to VCAT for an order requiring the renter to move out.

A rental provider cannot personally evict a renter.

Legal eviction requires the appropriate VCAT process and enforcement steps.

18. GOODS LEFT BEHIND

Renters should remove all personal belongings before leaving.

If goods are left behind, specific Victorian rules apply to how the rental provider must deal with those goods.

Renters should therefore arrange removal of their belongings before returning possession of the property.

19. CURRENT LAW AND FUTURE CHANGES

This document reflects Victorian rental information checked on 5 September 2026.

Rental laws can change over time.

The Consumer Affairs Victoria rental-law changes page identifies further changes scheduled for 13 October 2026.

Those future changes should not be treated as current requirements in this document.

20. RELEVANT LEGISLATION

Relevant provisions of the Residential Tenancies Act 1997 include:

- Section 91Z – Notice of intention to vacate
- Section 91ZA – Notice to have no effect in certain circumstances
- Section 91ZB – Reduced period of notice in certain circumstances
- Section 91ZZN – Form of notice of intention to vacate

Breaking a rental agreement is also governed by relevant provisions of the Residential Tenancies Act 1997.
