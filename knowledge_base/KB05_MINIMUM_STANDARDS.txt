SUMMARY
This document summarises the minimum standards that rental properties in Victoria must meet. Consumer Affairs Victoria identifies 15 categories of minimum standards. Rental providers and agents must ensure the property meets the standards when it is advertised or offered for rent and before the renter moves in.

1. MINIMUM STANDARD CATEGORIES

The 15 categories are:

- Bathroom
- Electrical safety
- Heating
- Kitchen
- Laundry
- Lighting
- Locks
- Mould and damp
- Structural soundness
- Toilets
- Ventilation
- Vermin-proof bins
- Windows
- Window coverings
- Window covering anchors

2. BATHROOM

The bathroom must have:

- A washbasin
- A shower or bath
- A reasonable supply of hot and cold water

Showers must generally have a shower head with a 3-star water efficiency rating.

If a 3-star shower head cannot be installed because of the property's age or another relevant reason, a 1- or 2-star shower head may be acceptable.

3. ELECTRICAL SAFETY

Rental properties must have modern switchboards with circuit breakers and electrical safety switches.

Rental providers are responsible for engaging an electrician to ensure the property complies with the electrical safety standard.

The electrical safety minimum standard commenced on 29 March 2023.

4. HEATING

A rental property must have a fixed heater in good working order in the main living area.

For rental agreements entered into from 29 March 2023, the fixed heater must meet the applicable energy-efficiency requirement.

An energy-efficient fixed heater can include:

- A non-ducted air conditioner or heat pump with a 2-star or higher energy rating
- A gas space heater with a 2-star or higher energy rating
- Ducted heating or hydronic heating with an outlet in the main living area
- A domestic solid-fuel appliance such as a fireplace or wood-burning stove

5. KITCHEN

A rental property must have a kitchen with:

- A dedicated cooking and food preparation area
- A sink in good working order
- A reasonable supply of hot and cold water
- A stovetop in good working order with at least two burners

If an oven is provided, it must be in good working order.

6. LAUNDRY

If the property has a laundry, it must be connected to a reasonable supply of hot and cold water.

7. LIGHTING

Interior rooms, corridors and hallways must have access to working lights.

The property must have access to natural light during the day.

At night, renters must have access to artificial light.

8. LOCKS

External entry doors, other than screen doors, must have functional deadlocks or locks that:

- Can be unlocked with a key from outside
- Can be opened from inside without a key

The locks must provide reasonable security against external entry.

9. MOULD AND DAMP

All rooms must be free from mould and damp caused by or related to the building structure.

Structural causes of mould or damp may therefore require action by the rental provider.

10. STRUCTURAL SOUNDNESS

The rental property must be structurally sound and weatherproof.

11. TOILETS

The toilet must:

- Be in good working order
- Be connected to an appropriate sewerage or wastewater system
- Be located in a separate room or an appropriate combined room such as a bathroom-laundry

12. VENTILATION

Rental properties must have adequate ventilation in all relevant rooms.

This includes:

- Habitable rooms
- Bathroom
- Shower
- Toilet
- Laundry

The applicable ventilation requirements can depend on the type of property and the Building Code of Australia.

13. VERMIN-PROOF BINS

Rental providers must supply rubbish and recycling bins for renters.

The bins must:

- Be vermin-proof
- Meet relevant council collection standards

14. WINDOWS

All external windows that can be opened must have functioning latches.

The latches must secure the windows against external entry.

Openable windows must also be able to remain in an open or closed position.

15. WINDOW COVERINGS

Rooms likely to be used as bedrooms or living areas must have curtains or blinds.

Window coverings must:

- Be capable of being closed
- Block light
- Provide privacy

16. WINDOW COVERING ANCHORS

From 1 December 2025, all corded internal window coverings, such as blinds and curtains, must have an anchor installed to secure the cords and prevent them from forming loops.

This requirement is a current minimum standard.

17. WHEN MINIMUM STANDARDS APPLY

Rental properties must meet the minimum standards before they are advertised or offered for rent.

For existing rental agreements, the minimum standards in Schedule 4 of the Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021 generally apply when the agreement:

- Started on or after 29 March 2021, or
- Started before 29 March 2021 and became a periodic agreement on or after 29 March 2021

Different rules can apply to rooming houses and other specialised accommodation.

18. IF A PROPERTY DOES NOT MEET THE STANDARDS

Renters should inspect a property before signing a rental agreement.

If the property does not meet the minimum standards, the renter can ask the rental provider to make repairs or changes before signing or moving in.

If the renter has already signed the agreement but has not moved in, the renter can generally:

- End the rental agreement, or
- Move in and request an urgent repair

If the renter is already living in the property, failure to meet a minimum standard may also amount to an urgent repair issue.

19. FUTURE ENERGY-EFFICIENCY CHANGES

New minimum rental standards relating to energy efficiency are scheduled to be introduced from 1 March 2027.

These future requirements should not be treated as current requirements in the knowledge base as of 4 September 2026.

20. RELEVANT LEGISLATION

The minimum standards are supported by the Residential Tenancies Act 1997 and the Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021.

Relevant provisions include:

- Section 65A – Occupation of rented premises that do not comply with minimum standards
- Section 65B – Advertisement of premises that do not comply with minimum standards
- Section 72 – Urgent repairs
- Section 77 – Payment of rent into Rent Special Account
- Schedule 4 of the Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021 – Rental minimum standards
