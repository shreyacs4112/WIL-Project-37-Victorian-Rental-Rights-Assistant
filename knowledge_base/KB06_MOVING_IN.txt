SUMMARY
This document summarises important steps and renter rights when moving into a residential rental property in Victoria. It focuses on minimum standards, condition reports, bonds and records that renters should keep when starting a rental agreement.

1. BEFORE MOVING IN

Before moving into a rental property, renters should check that the property meets the Victorian rental minimum standards.

Rental providers and agents are responsible for ensuring the property meets the applicable minimum standards when it is advertised or offered for rent and before the renter moves in.

If the property does not meet the minimum standards, the renter can ask the rental provider to make the required repairs or changes before signing the rental agreement or before moving in.

2. INFORMATION FROM THE RENTAL PROVIDER

Before a renter moves in, the rental provider or agent must provide relevant information and documents.

This can include:

- A copy of the rental agreement if it is in writing
- The Renters Guide
- A phone number for urgent repairs outside normal business hours
- The rental provider or agent's full name
- A postal address for sending documents
- An email address where electronic communication has been agreed
- A set of keys for each renter who signed the agreement
- A condition report

3. CONDITION REPORT

A condition report is a record of the property's condition at the beginning of a rental agreement.

It should record the condition of:

- Rooms
- Fittings
- Fixtures
- Items inside the property
- Items outside the property

Anything that is unclean, damaged or not working should be recorded.

4. CONDITION REPORT PROVIDED BY THE RENTAL PROVIDER

The rental provider or agent must prepare and sign the condition report.

They must provide the renter with:

- Two copies of the signed report before moving in, or
- One electronic copy when provided electronically

The renter should carefully inspect the property before completing their part of the report.

5. RENTER'S RESPONSIBILITY

The renter should check the condition report carefully and add comments where the report is incomplete or inaccurate.

The renter should record:

- Existing damage
- Marks or stains
- Broken or damaged fittings
- Items that are not working
- Areas that are not reasonably clean
- Other relevant defects or problems

The renter should then sign the completed report and return one copy to the rental provider or agent.

6. FIVE BUSINESS DAY DEADLINE

The renter must return the completed and signed condition report within 5 business days of moving into the property.

Weekends and public holidays are not counted as business days.

If the renter was not given a condition report before moving in, they can complete their own condition report and provide it to the rental provider or agent within 5 business days of moving in.

7. PHOTOGRAPHS AND EVIDENCE

Renters should take photographs of the property's condition when they move in.

Photographs are particularly useful for documenting pre-existing damage.

Photos should be:

- Dated
- Clearly labelled
- Kept with the condition report

Digital photographs can be used.

Keeping photographic evidence can help resolve later disputes about damage, cleaning or bond claims.

8. IMPORTANCE OF THE CONDITION REPORT

The condition report can be important evidence if there is a dispute about:

- Bond claims
- Cleaning
- Property damage
- Missing items
- Responsibility for repairs

Renters should keep their copy of the completed condition report for the entire rental agreement.

The rental provider or agent should also retain their copy.

9. REPAIRS IDENTIFIED IN THE CONDITION REPORT

If a condition report identifies something that needs repair, the report can provide written notice of the issue to the rental provider.

If the required repair is not completed within a reasonable period, the renter may be able to apply to VCAT for an order requiring the repair.

Urgent repairs are subject to separate rules and should be reported as soon as possible.

10. IF THE PROPERTY DOES NOT MEET MINIMUM STANDARDS

If the property does not meet the minimum rental standards before the renter moves in, the renter can request that the rental provider make the necessary repairs or changes.

If the rental agreement has already been signed but the renter has not moved in, the renter can generally:

- End the rental agreement immediately without fees by notifying the rental provider that the property does not meet minimum standards, or
- Move in and request urgent repairs

If a property falls below the minimum standards during the rental agreement, the renter can contact the rental provider or agent and request an urgent repair.

11. BOND WHEN MOVING IN

A rental provider or agent may require a renter to pay a bond, subject to Victorian rental law.

The maximum bond is generally one month's rent unless the weekly rent is more than $900.

In some circumstances, the rental provider may apply to VCAT for an increase to the bond limit.

12. LODGING THE BOND

If the renter pays the bond to the rental provider or agent, it must generally be lodged with the Residential Tenancies Bond Authority (RTBA) within 10 business days of the rental provider or agent receiving it.

The RTBA holds the bond in trust until it is claimed.

The RTBA sends the renter a receipt containing the bond number.

Renters should keep their bond number in a safe place.

If a renter does not receive a bond receipt within 15 business days of making payment, they should contact the RTBA.

13. ELECTRONIC COMMUNICATION

Renters and rental providers can agree to exchange certain documents and information electronically.

This can include:

- Rental agreements
- Condition reports
- Information about rental rights
- Inspection notices
- Other notices and documents

Where electronic communication is used, the parties should keep copies of important documents.

14. RECORD KEEPING

Renters should retain important records from the beginning of the tenancy, including:

- Rental agreement
- Condition report
- Photographs
- Bond receipt and bond number
- Rent records
- Repair requests
- Communications with the rental provider or agent
- Notices received

These records can be useful if a dispute occurs later.

15. CONDITION REPORT AT THE END OF THE AGREEMENT

When the rental agreement ends, the rental provider or agent must complete the exit condition report section of the original condition report within 10 days.

The renter must be present or have been given a reasonable opportunity to attend.

The exit condition report records the condition of the property at the end of the tenancy.

16. CONDITION REPORT AND BOND DISPUTES

The original condition report and photographs can be used as evidence when determining responsibility for damage or cleaning.

A rental provider may use the condition report to support a bond claim for new damage, repairs, additional cleaning or missing items.

Normal fair wear and tear should be distinguished from new damage.

17. RELEVANT LEGISLATION

Condition reports are covered by relevant provisions of the Residential Tenancies Act 1997.

Consumer Affairs Victoria identifies sections including:

- Section 35
- Section 97
- Section 148
- Section 206O
