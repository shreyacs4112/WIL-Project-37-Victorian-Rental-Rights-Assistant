SUMMARY
This document summarises Victorian rental rules relating to repairs in rental properties. Repairs are classified as either urgent or non-urgent. Rental providers are responsible for repairs that are not caused by the renter.

1. URGENT AND NON-URGENT REPAIRS

Urgent repairs are repairs that make a rental property unsafe or difficult to live in and are legally defined under Victorian rental law.

Non-urgent repairs are repairs that are not classified as urgent. A repair is generally non-urgent when the renter can continue to safely live in the property.

Examples of non-urgent repairs can include a broken dishwasher or loose bathroom tiles.

Rental providers must arrange urgent repairs immediately. Non-urgent repairs must generally be completed within 14 days after receiving a written repair request.

2. EXAMPLES OF URGENT REPAIRS

The Consumer Affairs Victoria urgent-repair list includes issues such as:

- Burst water services
- Blocked or broken toilet systems
- Serious roof leaks
- Gas leaks
- Dangerous electrical faults
- Flooding or serious flood damage
- Serious storm or fire damage
- Failure of essential hot water, water, cooking, heating or laundering services/appliances
- Failure of gas, electricity or water supply
- Failure of a cooling appliance or service provided by the rental provider
- The property not meeting minimum rental standards
- A non-working safety device such as a smoke alarm or pool fence
- Certain appliances, fittings or fixtures causing significant water wastage
- Faults or damage making the property unsafe or insecure, including certain structural pests, mould or damp issues
- Serious problems with a lift or staircase

3. REPORTING REPAIRS

Renters should notify the rental provider or property manager in writing about repairs as soon as possible.

For urgent repairs, the renter should contact the rental provider or agent immediately, including using the emergency contact number provided when they moved in. The renter should also confirm the request in writing and keep evidence of the request.

For non-urgent repairs, the request should be in writing and include the date of the request.

4. WHO PAYS FOR REPAIRS

The rental provider generally organises and pays for repairs when the damage or fault was not caused by the renter.

If the renter caused the damage, the renter may be responsible for the repair costs.

Where repairs require a licence or registration, the work must be completed by a suitably qualified person. Repairs must be completed to the standard expected of a tradesperson.

5. URGENT REPAIRS PAID FOR BY THE RENTER

If a rental provider or agent does not respond promptly to an urgent repair request, a renter may arrange and pay for the repair themselves when the repair costs $2,500 or less.

The renter should keep receipts and provide written notice requesting reimbursement. The rental provider must reimburse the renter within 7 days of receiving the written notice.

For urgent repairs costing more than $2,500, the renter should not arrange and pay for the repair themselves. They should seek assistance through Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria (RDRV).

6. NON-URGENT REPAIRS NOT COMPLETED

If a rental provider does not complete a non-urgent repair within 14 days of the written request, the renter has options to seek further assistance.

A renter can request a repairs inspection from Consumer Affairs Victoria. The renter may also apply to VCAT if the required repairs have not been completed within the relevant timeframe.

7. RENT PAYMENTS DURING REPAIRS

Renters must continue paying rent while waiting for repairs to be completed or while waiting for reimbursement for repairs.

In some circumstances, a renter can apply for rent to be paid into a Rent Special Account while a repair-related issue is being resolved.

8. WHEN THE RENTER CAUSED THE DAMAGE

If the renter caused a fault or damage, the rental provider can require the renter to repair the damage or pay for the repair.

The rental provider must provide a written repair notice explaining the damage, that the renter caused it, and whether the renter is expected to arrange the repair or whether the rental provider will arrange it.

If the renter is required to organise the repair, it generally must be completed within 14 days and to a tradesperson standard.

9. DISPUTE RESOLUTION

Renters and rental providers should first try to resolve repair issues by communicating with each other.

Consumer Affairs Victoria provides a free repairs inspection service in relevant circumstances.

Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria (RDRV) is a free alternative dispute resolution service that can assist with rental disputes.

If a dispute cannot be resolved through RDRV, the matter may proceed to VCAT where appropriate.
